# **1\. Product Overview**

The Academic Ecosystem is a trusted digital environment connecting universities, faculties, departments, verified lecturers, and verified students around legitimate academic content and meaningful learning activity. It is designed to replace fragmented material distribution and informal information channels with an organized, lecturer-owned academic experience.

The product is not intended to be a generic PDF marketplace. Its primary unit of value is the academic relationship: institution → department → course → lecturer → student. Materials, assessments, announcements, Q\&A, study progress, AI assistance, rewards, and lecturer insights all sit inside that relationship.

| Outcome | What the product enables |
| :---- | :---- |
| Trust | Students can identify verified lecturers, official courses and officially published materials. |
| Affordability | Students can access selected paid resources at controlled, student-friendly prices. |
| Engagement | Lecturers and students have a structured academic communication and Q\&A environment. |
| Study continuity | Students maintain a personal academic library and a long-term study journey. |
| Lecturer participation | Lecturers can publish, communicate, review engagement, and earn transparently. |
| Learning assistance | AI helps students understand and research authorized course materials first. |
| Shared identity | The same daily Word/devotional experience appears to every student. |

# **2\. Problem Statement**

1\. Students often depend on class representatives, informal groups, tutorial sellers, and unverified sources for lecture notes and academic updates.

2\. Unofficial or altered PDFs can circulate without a reliable way for students to know whether a resource is genuine or current.

3\. Students can be charged excessive amounts for notes, tutorials, and study packs that are difficult to validate.

4\. Lecturers have limited visibility into whether students are accessing their materials, how students perceive those materials, and where students are struggling.

5\. Students lack a unified record of their study activity, purchased academic resources, lecturer interactions, and learning milestones.

6\. Generic AI tools can produce useful explanations but may not distinguish the lecturer’s approved material from broader, potentially unrelated information.

7\. Academic announcements, tests, assignments, materials, and Q\&A are often scattered across unrelated communication channels.

8\. There is an opportunity to encourage sustained study behaviour through a controlled, non-cash reward system.

# **3\. Product Vision and Principles**

Vision: Create a trusted academic ecosystem where students can study from legitimate lecturer resources, remain connected to their courses, receive timely information, track their academic journey, and receive learning support—while lecturers gain a structured environment to teach, engage, measure impact, and earn from eligible resources.

## **3.1 Core principles**

·       Academic authority first: the lecturer is the owner of their course content and the institution establishes academic affiliation.

·       Trusted by design: official materials, verified lecturers, and course ownership should be visible and understandable to students.

·       Academic value over document volume: success should come from useful learning activity, not maximizing PDF purchases.

·       Affordable access: paid resources must remain within platform-approved boundaries that respect student affordability.

·       The Word at the center: the same authorized daily devotional experience is presented to every student.

·       AI as an assistant, not the lecturer: AI supports learning but does not replace the lecturer as the academic authority.

·       Long-term academic continuity: legitimate access and study history should support future reference where the material’s access rules allow it.

·       No uncontrolled private classroom: lecturer communication is based on official announcements and course Q\&A.

# **4\. Target Users and Roles**

| Role | Primary responsibilities / needs |
| :---- | :---- |
| Student | Join verified institution, access approved courses, study materials, attend tests/assignments, ask questions, earn points, use AI, and track progress. |
| Lecturer | Manage courses, upload and approve resources, publish announcements, respond to Q\&A, review engagement, receive material feedback, and earn from eligible materials. |
| Department / Institution administrator | Support verification of lecturers and students, manage academic structures, and exercise appropriate institutional oversight. |
| Platform administrator | Protect the integrity of the ecosystem, review content, manage disputes, oversee financial/reward rules, and enforce platform policies. |

# **5\. Product Structure**

The core academic hierarchy should be clear and consistent:

**University → Faculty → Department → Level → Course → Lecturer → Student**

## **5.1 Access model**

·       Students are verified as members of an institution before they receive broader academic access.

·       A verified student does not automatically gain access to every course; lecturers control access to their own courses.

·       Lecturers are verified through institutional/departmental confirmation plus platform verification.

·       Lecturers operate within a dedicated lecturer environment and do not use it as a student account.

·       The student experience centers on the courses, resources, announcements and Q\&A that are authorized for that student.

# **6\. Student Experience and Requirements**

## **6.1 Student home screen**

The home screen prioritizes academic activity while keeping the daily Word as the central experience.

1\. Today’s Word / devotional experience — the same official daily devotional for every student.

2\. Academic priorities — tests, assignments, deadlines, urgent lecturer announcements and newly released materials.

3\. Continue studying — returns the student to the current material or study activity.

4\. My Courses — current courses with visible study progress.

5\. Lecturer updates — recent official announcements.

6\. Course Q\&A — unanswered questions and recent lecturer responses.

7\. Academic points — current balance and long-term reward progress.

8\. AI Study Assistant — direct access to course-grounded learning support.

## **6.2 Student academic profile**

·       Verified institution, faculty, department and level.

·       Current and completed courses.

·       Study progress and milestones.

·       Purchased materials and eligible access status.

·       Academic points and reward level.

·       Lecturer recognitions and participation milestones.

·       Historical study journey across academic levels.

## **6.3 Personal Academic Library**

Students should have a single place to find resources they are legitimately authorized to use. The library is not a downloadable file store; materials remain accessible only within the ecosystem.

·       Purchased materials.

·       Free official course materials.

·       Lecturer-created bundles.

·       Previously authorized editions where the stated access policy permits continued access.

·       Clear access status and access-duration information before and after purchase.

# **7\. Lecturer Experience and Requirements**

## **7.1 Lecturer dashboard**

·       Courses and enrolled students.

·       Published, draft and reviewed materials.

·       Material access, completion and review signals.

·       Announcements and Q\&A management.

·       Test and assignment activity.

·       Student engagement and academic participation.

·       Lecturer activity and contribution indicators.

·       Earnings, pending balance, available balance and settlement history.

·       AI-generated insights that summarize meaningful trends and areas requiring attention.

## **7.2 Lecturer course control**

·       Approve or manage student access to the lecturer’s course.

·       Publish official announcements.

·       Create and manage course Q\&A.

·       Create tests, quizzes and assignments.

·       Upload, price and classify eligible academic resources.

·       Set permitted access duration for paid resources within platform rules.

·       Review student feedback on materials.

# **8\. Academic Content and Material Lifecycle**

## **8.1 Supported material types**

·       Lecture notes and course packs.

·       Revision guides.

·       Practice question collections.

·       Exam preparation materials.

·       Lecturer-created study guides and summaries.

·       Other institutionally appropriate academic resources approved under the platform rules.

## **8.2 Free versus paid content**

| Free / essential | Paid / optional value-added |
| :---- | :---- |
| Course outline | Detailed lecture notes |
| Lecturer announcements | Comprehensive revision packs |
| Assignment instructions | Practice question collections |
| Examination information | Advanced study guides |
| Essential course guidance | Additional lecturer-created study resources |

&nbsp;

## **8.3 Pricing model**

Lecturers can price eligible paid materials within platform-approved boundaries. The platform may also support lecturer-created bundles. Essential course information remains free.

## **8.4 Material lifecycle**

**Draft → Lecturer approval → Platform review → Published → Reviewed / Updated → Archived**

·       The lecturer confirms ownership and authorization of the material before submission.

·       The platform reviews the material against its publishing rules before it becomes official within the ecosystem.

·       Students see a clear distinction between official lecturer-published material and other platform information.

·       Materials can be revised and versioned rather than silently replaced.

·       A purchase never unexpectedly changes into a different access arrangement; access terms must be visible before payment.

## **8.5 Access rules**

·       All products and materials remain accessible only inside the ecosystem.

·       The product does not provide unrestricted download, offline access, screenshot/capture tools, or export of protected materials; supported in-app copying/capture should be restricted where applicable, while external-device capture cannot be guaranteed.

·       Access duration is lecturer-defined within platform rules and must be clearly shown before purchase.

·       Permanent or long-term access may be offered for appropriate materials, supporting future academic reference.

·       Protected resources should carry clear ownership/official-content indicators.

# **9\. Communication and Engagement**

## **9.1 Official announcements**

Lecturers communicate course-critical information through an organized announcement channel. Announcements are retained inside the course and can be categorized for clarity.

·       New material

·       Assignment

·       Test / examination

·       Course notice

·       General academic update

·       Urgent update

## **9.2 Course Q\&A**

·       Students ask academic questions in the course space.

·       Lecturers answer questions for the benefit of the whole class.

·       Lecturer responses are clearly distinguished from student responses.

·       Questions can move from unanswered to answered/resolved.

·       Students can search historical Q\&A as a course knowledge resource.

·       There is no unrestricted one-to-one student/lecturer chat in the approved scope.

# **10\. Study Journey and Academic Tracking**

The Study Journey is the student’s long-term academic record inside the ecosystem. It should show progression rather than simply count document openings.

·       Courses taken and completed.

·       Meaningful material progress and completion.

·       Tests, quizzes and assignments completed.

·       Academic milestones and study streaks where appropriate.

·       Course Q\&A participation.

·       Lecturer recognitions.

·       Academic points earned over time.

·       Purchased resources retained in the student’s academic library according to their access terms.

## **10.1 Tests, quizzes and assignments**

Lecturers can create assessments. Objective elements can be graded by the platform, while subjective work can remain lecturer-reviewed. Assessment completion and meaningful academic activity can feed the study journey and points system.

# **11\. AI Learning Experience**

The AI is a learning assistant grounded first in the student’s authorized academic environment.

## **11.1 AI source hierarchy**

1\. Primary: authorized lecturer/course materials the student is permitted to access.

2\. Secondary: broader academic knowledge, clearly separated and labeled as additional context.

3\. The AI must not present general AI output as the lecturer’s official position.

## **11.2 Student AI capabilities**

·       Explain difficult concepts in simpler language.

·       Summarize authorized materials.

·       Compare concepts across authorized course resources.

·       Answer questions about a specific material through an “Ask This Material” experience.

·       Generate study questions and practice activities based on authorized materials.

·       Run quiz/revision sessions.

·       Support exam preparation and study planning using available course information.

## **11.3 Lecturer AI insights**

·       Highlight meaningful changes in student engagement.

·       Identify frequently asked or unresolved topics.

·       Highlight resources with strong or weak completion signals.

·       Summarize student activity and emerging areas of difficulty.

·       Suggest areas where a lecturer may want to publish clarification or additional material.

# **12\. Academic Points and Rewards**

Academic Points are a non-cash ecosystem reward. They are designed to reinforce sustained study behaviour and meaningful academic participation. They are intentionally difficult and slow to accumulate so they do not become a substitute for normal student payments.

## **12.1 Earning points**

·       The system awards points for defined, measurable academic milestones.

·       Lecturers can award limited recognition points for genuine participation, strong answers, improvement, or other approved academic contributions.

·       Points are not awarded simply because a student opens a resource or makes a purchase.

·       Points should be subject to sensible caps and anti-abuse rules.

## **12.2 Gradable reward structure**

The points economy should use high redemption thresholds. Small amounts of points can accumulate slowly, while meaningful purchase benefits require sustained academic activity over a long period.

| Reward level | Purpose |
| :---- | :---- |
| Early milestone | Recognizes first meaningful study achievements without creating purchasing power too quickly. |
| Intermediate milestone | Rewards sustained engagement across multiple academic activities. |
| Advanced milestone | Requires substantial, consistent study activity before producing a meaningful purchase benefit. |
| Long-term milestone | Reserved for students who sustain engagement over a significant period. |

&nbsp;

## **12.3 Redemption**

·       Points may be used for eligible purchases within the ecosystem.

·       Points cannot be withdrawn as cash.

·       Points cannot be treated as lecturer earnings or eSpees.

·       The amount of points required for redemption should remain high enough to preserve platform economics.

# **13\. Payments, eSpees and Lecturer Earnings**

## **13.1 Three-value model**

| Value type | Purpose | Cash-out |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Naira | Student payments for eligible purchases and other approved charges. | Actual payment value. |
| eSpees | Internal ecosystem settlement/accounting value for lecturer earnings and approved ecosystem transactions. | Lecturer settlement subject to platform rules and approved settlement periods. |
| Academic Points | Student academic reward for meaningful study and participation. | No cash withdrawal. |

&nbsp;

## **13.2 Purchase flow**

1\. Student views the material, price and access terms.

2\. Student completes payment in the approved payment flow.

3\. The purchase is recorded in the student’s academic library.

4\. The lecturer’s earnings enter a pending state according to platform rules.

5\. The appropriate lecturer share and platform allocation are recorded transparently.

## **13.3 Lecturer earnings dashboard**

·       Material-level sales and access summary.

·       Lecturer share versus platform allocation.

·       Pending earnings.

·       Available earnings.

·       Settlement history.

·       Next scheduled settlement period.

# **14\. Devotional Experience**

The Word of God is a central part of the student home experience and is intentionally the same for every student on a given day.

·       Every student sees the same daily Word/devotional content for that day.

·       The official devotional source should be used only through an appropriate authorized arrangement.

·       The platform may provide a separate, clearly labeled academic reflection connected to learning themes such as discipline, excellence, integrity or consistency.

·       Previous daily devotional content can remain accessible through a devotional archive, subject to applicable content rights.

·       The devotional experience is visible on the home screen without displacing urgent academic responsibilities.

# **15\. Reviews, Trust and Academic Integrity**

## **15.1 Material reviews**

·       Students who have meaningfully accessed/completed an eligible resource can review it.

·       Reviews may include a rating and written feedback.

·       Lecturers can respond to feedback where appropriate.

·       Students can report material problems or possible violations.

·       A report does not automatically remove a lecturer’s material; it enters a defined review process.

## **15.2 Trust indicators**

·       Verified Institution

·       Verified Lecturer

·       Official Course

·       Officially Published Material

·       Material edition/version information

·       Clear access and purchase terms

## **15.3 Academic integrity and misuse**

The ecosystem should clearly prohibit unauthorized redistribution, impersonation, fraudulent academic identity, abusive use of the reward system, and misuse of lecturer-owned resources. The platform should provide an understandable reporting and escalation process.

# **16\. Platform Governance and Administration**

| Level | Primary authority |
| :---- | :---- |
| Platform administration | Platform integrity, content review rules, disputes, financial/reward policy, user governance. |
| Institution / department | Academic affiliation, lecturer verification, student structure and institutional oversight. |
| Lecturer | Course ownership, course access, academic content, announcements, Q\&A, assessments, student recognition. |
| Student | Study, participate, purchase eligible resources, ask questions, provide reviews, and follow ecosystem policies. |

&nbsp;

## **16.1 Dispute model**

1\. A user submits a report or dispute.

2\. The responsible party is notified and given an opportunity to provide context or correction.

3\. The appropriate review level assesses the issue.

4\. A resolution is recorded and communicated.

5\. Where appropriate, a defined appeal/review path exists.

# **17\. Notifications and Email**

Notifications should support academic activity without becoming noisy. In-app notifications are the primary surface; email is reserved for meaningful events that benefit from an additional reminder or record.

## **17.1 Important notification events**

·       New official lecturer announcement.

·       New material published or purchased.

·       Payment confirmation.

·       Purchase receipt/order confirmation.

·       Test or examination update.

·       Assignment deadline reminder.

·       Course access approval or status change.

·       Material revision affecting an existing authorized resource.

·       Important lecturer or platform account notice.

# **18\. Product Rules and Guardrails**

| Rule | Product expectation |
| :---- | :---- |
| Protected ecosystem | All products/materials remain accessible only within the ecosystem. |
| No unrestricted file distribution | The product does not provide normal download, offline, screenshot/capture, or export access to protected materials. |
| Verified identity | Students and lecturers must meet the applicable verification rules. |
| Lecturer course authority | Lecturers control access to their individual courses. |
| Lecturer ownership | Lecturers explicitly authorize their uploaded resources. |
| Platform review | Published material passes platform publishing review. |
| Transparent pricing | Students see price and access terms before purchase. |
| No cash-out points | Academic points cannot be withdrawn as money. |
| Slow points economy | Points require sustained study activity and are subject to caps. |
| No unrestricted private chat | Communication uses announcements and course Q\&A. |
| AI source clarity | Course-grounded answers are distinguished from broader AI knowledge. |
| Fair dispute handling | Reports and disputes use a defined review process rather than arbitrary removal. |

# **19\. Success Measures**

The product should be evaluated on whether it creates a healthier academic ecosystem, not only on transactions.

| Area | Illustrative measures |
| :---- | :---- |
| Student adoption | Verified students active; students returning weekly; course participation. |
| Academic engagement | Material completion, assessment participation, Q\&A participation, study milestones. |
| Lecturer adoption | Verified lecturers active; materials published; announcement and Q\&A activity. |
| Content trust | Share of content carrying official/verified status; material feedback; reported-content resolution. |
| Affordability | Student purchase frequency, average paid-resource price, bundle usage, point redemption usage. |
| Learning support | AI study sessions, material-grounded queries, practice usage, student satisfaction. |
| Ecosystem health | Repeat participation, retention, low abuse rate, dispute resolution quality. |
| Lecturer sustainability | Eligible sales, transparent earnings, publishing consistency, engagement with student questions. |

# **20\. MVP / Release Phasing**

The product should be introduced in manageable stages, protecting the central value proposition instead of attempting to launch every capability at once.

## **Phase 1 — Trusted academic foundation**

·       Institution, department, course, lecturer and student structure.

·       Student and lecturer verification.

·       Lecturer course spaces and student course access.

·       Official announcements and course Q\&A.

·       Material upload, lecturer approval, platform review and protected in-ecosystem access.

·       Individual material purchases and bundles.

·       Basic academic library and study progress.

## **Phase 2 — Engagement and academic economy**

·       Tests, quizzes and assignments.

·       Academic Points with controlled lecturer recognition.

·       Lecturer earnings dashboard and scheduled settlement workflow.

·       Material reviews and structured reporting.

·       Expanded notifications and email events.

## **Phase 3 — AI and advanced insights**

·       Course-grounded student AI assistant.

·       Ask This Material.

·       Study, quiz, revision and research modes.

·       Lecturer AI insights and engagement summaries.

·       Deeper Study Journey analytics.

## **Phase 4 — Shared daily Word experience and ecosystem expansion**

·       Authorized official daily devotional experience.

·       Daily Word-centered home design for every student.

·       Clearly separated academic reflection layer.

·       Cross-institution expansion and broader academic ecosystem capabilities.

# **Product Definition Summary**

**A trusted academic ecosystem — not a PDF marketplace.**

The final product concept is a verified, lecturer-led academic ecosystem organized around departments and courses. Students receive a shared daily Word experience and a personalized academic journey. Lecturers gain a controlled publishing environment, meaningful engagement with students, academic analytics, and a transparent earning pathway. The ecosystem uses protected in-platform access, affordable paid resources, controlled Academic Points, eSpees settlement, course-grounded AI assistance, and structured announcements/Q\&A to create a coherent experience from learning to long-term academic reference.
